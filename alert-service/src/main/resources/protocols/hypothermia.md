# Protocol 6.2: Hypothermia (low body temperature)

DEMO CONTENT for the CareVoice demo of Sunrise Care, a fictional care home. Not clinical guidance.

## When this protocol applies
A hypothermia alert is raised when the body temperature is below 35.0 °C. Older residents lose heat easily, and a
low body temperature can also be a sign of serious illness.

## Immediate steps
- Remeasure the body temperature with a different thermometer or method.
- Warm the resident gradually: dry clothing, blankets, a warm room and warm drinks if the resident can swallow safely.
- Do not use hot water bottles or heating pads directly on the skin.
- Measure pulse and blood pressure.

## When to escalate
- If the resident is drowsy, confused, shivering strongly or has a slow pulse, call emergency services.
- If the low temperature is confirmed without these signs, inform the physician immediately.

## Documentation
- Document every reading with the time and method, the warming measures and the resident's response.
- Recheck the body temperature every 30 minutes until it is back in the normal range.
