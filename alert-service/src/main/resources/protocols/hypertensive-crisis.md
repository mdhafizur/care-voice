# Protocol 4.2: Hypertensive crisis (very high blood pressure)

DEMO CONTENT for the CareVoice demo of Sunrise Care, a fictional care home. Not clinical guidance.

## When this protocol applies
A hypertensive crisis alert is raised when the systolic blood pressure is 180 mmHg or higher, or the diastolic blood
pressure is 120 mmHg or higher. Very high blood pressure needs a prompt response, but a single reading can be wrong.

## Immediate steps
- Recheck the blood pressure after five minutes of rest, seated, on the other arm, with a correctly sized cuff.
- Ask the resident about headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, blurred vision, confusion or weakness on one side.
- Stay with the resident and keep them calm and seated.

## When to escalate
- If the repeated reading is still 180/120 mmHg or higher and the resident has any warning sign, call emergency
  services immediately and then inform the physician.
- If the repeated reading is still very high without warning signs, inform the on-call physician within 30 minutes
  and follow their orders.
- Inform the ward lead in both cases.

## Documentation
- Document both readings, the time, the arm used and the symptoms you asked about.
- Recheck the blood pressure every 15 minutes until the physician has decided on next steps.
