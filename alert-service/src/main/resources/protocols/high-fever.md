# Protocol 6.1: High fever (elevated body temperature)

DEMO CONTENT for the CareVoice demo of Sunrise Care, a fictional care home. Not clinical guidance.

## When this protocol applies
A high fever alert is raised when the body temperature is 39.5 °C or higher. In older residents a fever can be a sign
of an infection such as pneumonia or a urinary tract infection, and it can lead to dehydration and confusion.

## Immediate steps
- Remeasure the body temperature with the same method after 15 minutes.
- Measure pulse, blood pressure and, if available, respiratory rate.
- Ask about cough, shortness of breath, pain when passing urine, new confusion or reduced drinking.
- Offer fluids and remove excess blankets; keep the room comfortably cool.

## When to escalate
- If the resident is very drowsy, newly confused, short of breath or has low blood pressure, call emergency services.
- Otherwise inform the physician within the shift; a fever in a resident always needs a physician's review.

## Documentation
- Document every temperature reading with the time and method, the other vital signs, fluid intake and symptoms.
- Recheck the temperature every four hours until the physician has reviewed the resident.
